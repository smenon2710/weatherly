Privacy Policy — SkySpeak

Last updated: October 27, 2026

SkySpeak: Premium Weather Chat ("SkySpeak", "the app") is a weather application developed by an independent solo developer. This policy explains what data the app accesses, how it is used, and who it is shared with. SkySpeak has no backend server of its own — it is a purely client-side app that talks directly to the third-party services listed below.

Background location access

By default, SkySpeak only accesses your location while you have the app open, to show you the forecast for where you are. Two optional features, both off by default and found in Settings, use your location in the background as well:

  • Alert Notifications — notifies you when a severe or extreme National Weather Service advisory becomes active for your current location, and again when it ends.
  • Weather Status Notification — keeps an ongoing notification in your notification shade showing current conditions for your location, updated periodically.

Turning on either feature shows an in-app explanation of this background use, followed by a separate Android system permission prompt for "Allow all the time" location access — granting your regular while-using-the-app location permission alone is not enough, and the app will never request background access unless you first opt into one of these two features. When enabled, the app checks your current location roughly every 30 minutes, in the background, using Android's WorkManager (so it can run periodically without keeping the app itself active). Both features can be turned off at any time in Settings, which stops the background location checks entirely; nothing about this is required for the rest of the app to work.

The home-screen widget, if you add one, also looks up your current location when it updates, to show the weather where you are. It sends that location to the same services as the app. Without "Allow all the time", Android may not give the widget a location while the app is closed; it then shows the most recent forecast the app saved.

Third-party services

Because SkySpeak has no backend of its own, the data above is sent directly from your device to these services:

  • Open-Meteo — receives your location coordinates (including in the background, if you've enabled one of the notification features above) to return forecast, air quality, and location-search results. See Open-Meteo's terms.
  • U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) — receives your location coordinates (including in the background, if you've enabled either notification feature above) to check for active official weather advisories (e.g. severe weather warnings, air quality alerts) in your area. This is a free, public U.S. government service; only U.S. locations receive advisory data, since the Service does not cover other countries. See the National Weather Service privacy policy.
  • NOAA Tides & Currents (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov, part of the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration) — for coastal U.S. locations only, receives the ID of the nearest NOAA tide station (within about 10 km of the location) to return today's tide times. Your coordinates are not sent; the nearest station is worked out on your phone from a list built into the app. For inland and non-U.S. locations, NOAA is not contacted.
  • Your phone's geocoding service — when the app, the widget or a background check shows weather for your current location, it asks Android's built-in geocoder for the place name (e.g. "Franklin Park, New Jersey"), sending the coordinates to it. On most Android phones this service is provided by Google. Places you search for or save already have a name and are not looked up this way.
  • OpenRouter — powers the AI chat. The app from Google Play includes a built-in key, so the chat works without you entering one; you can also enter your own OpenRouter key in Settings. When a question goes to the AI model, the text you type, your recent messages in that conversation, and your current forecast (including the place name) are sent to OpenRouter and forwarded to the underlying AI model to generate a response. The quick-suggestion buttons and simple everyday questions (umbrella, jacket, what to wear, walking, driving, hiking) are answered on your phone and are not sent. If you never use the chat, nothing is sent to OpenRouter. See OpenRouter's privacy policy.

SkySpeak does not sell your data, and does not share it with any party for advertising or marketing purposes.

Data stored on your device

The following is kept only in the app's local storage on your phone and never transmitted to the developer:

  • Your selected unit system (metric/imperial) and saved locations
  • The most recent successful forecast, cached so the app opens instantly offline
  • Your OpenRouter API key, if you choose to enter one in Settings (only ever sent to OpenRouter itself, when a chat question goes to the AI model)
  • A count of today's AI chat requests (for the daily limit on the built-in key) and the list of weather alerts last seen (so the app can tell you when one ends)

Uninstalling the app removes all of this data from your phone. If Android backup is turned on for your device, Android may also include the app's settings and saved data in that backup (to your Google account, or when moving to a new phone) so they can be restored after a reinstall; that backup is managed in your Android or Google account settings, and the developer has no access to it. There is no cloud account or sync of the app's own, so there is nothing for the developer to delete on your behalf.

Children's privacy

SkySpeak is a general-audience weather utility and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.
